# Paid Pilot Delivery Board

The Paid Pilot Delivery Board turns close-room momentum into an operating path for the first paid week. It exists because a strong paid-pilot ask is not the same thing as a delivered paid pilot. Once MajlisAlpha has proof, close odds, and a named paid lane, the founder still needs to confirm scope, invoice or paid plan, kickoff question, source spine, first paid brief, review cadence, and renewal signal.

The board reads the Pilot Close Room, Pilot Conversion Pipeline, Pilot Follow-Up Board, source records, evidence ledger, and current brief state. It produces a delivery score, delivery cards, and a day-by-day timeline that identifies the next operational gap.

## Operating Flow

1. Open the Pilot Close Room and confirm close odds, paid ask, and primary objection.
2. Open the Paid Pilot Delivery Board.
3. Check whether the paid lane is named and whether invoice or paid plan exists.
4. Confirm the first paid workflow and kickoff question.
5. Attach at least one source spine or verified evidence item.
6. Run the first paid-pilot question and export the brief.
7. Schedule the day-7 review so renewal, expansion, or churn risk becomes visible.

## Delivery Signals

- Scope readiness comes from the close-room ask or conversion close action.
- Invoice readiness comes from a paid-pilot stage, won stage, AED 199, AED 399, or team invoice lane.
- Kickoff readiness comes from a session or follow-up motion.
- Source readiness comes from source-pack records, uploaded documents, or verified ledger evidence.
- First paid brief readiness comes from a current answer with citations.
- Renewal readiness comes from revenue proof, won stage, or a dated review.

## Practical Rule

Do not call a pilot "paid-ready" only because the customer liked the demo. The first paid week needs one workflow, one source spine, one deliverable, one owner, and one review date. The board should push the founder back to follow-up, source workspace, desk run, or conversion pipeline whenever those pieces are missing.
