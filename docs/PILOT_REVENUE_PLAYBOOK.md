# MajlisAlpha Pilot Revenue Playbook

MajlisAlpha should not wait for a perfect data pipeline before learning whether the UAE market wants the product. The pilot is designed to test one practical question: will serious UAE market users pay for cited disclosure answers, real-source imports, watchlists, and committee-ready research exports?

## Pilot Goal

Run a focused 14-day pilot with 10 serious users. The pilot is successful only if users bring real UAE market questions, upload or paste official source material, repeat usage without being pushed, and show paid intent.

Primary target:

- 10 pilot users.
- 50 real UAE market questions.
- 15 uploaded or pasted source packs.
- 3 paid-intent conversations.
- 0 trust failures in reviewed memo output.

## Who To Invite First

Start with people who already feel the pain of reading UAE disclosures:

- Active UAE public-market investors who compare ADX and DFM issuers.
- GCC family-office analysts who need fast first-pass diligence.
- Finance creators or newsletter operators who publish UAE market commentary.
- IR, advisory, or capital-markets operators who monitor issuer messaging.

Avoid broad consumer outreach at this stage. The product needs demanding users who can judge whether the source workflow is credible.

## Demo Flow

Use one question from the user's real workflow. Do not demo with a generic prompt unless they are stuck.

1. Ask the question inside the desk.
2. Show citation cards and source labels.
3. Open the company dossier.
4. Run the Source Intake Doctor on a pasted official note or source URL.
5. Export a brief.
6. Record the review decision.
7. Ask whether the workflow saves enough time or improves enough trust to justify paid access.

## Pilot Offer

Use pricing as a learning instrument, not as a final packaging decision.

- Pro Pilot: AED 199 per month for saved briefs, real-source imports, watchlists, and cited UAE disclosure answers.
- Desk Evaluation: AED 399 per month for review logs, committee-ready exports, source packs, and repeatable issuer coverage.

If users say the price is fine but do not ask to keep access, treat that as weak signal. Paid intent is stronger than praise.

## Conversion Signals

Green signals:

- The user asks a second or third question without prompting.
- The user uploads or pastes a real source pack.
- The user asks to save a watchlist or recurring coverage list.
- The user uses export, PDF, or committee wording.
- The user asks about price, team access, or launch timing.

Yellow signals:

- The user likes the interface but does not bring real work.
- The user wants broad coverage before testing one issuer properly.
- The user asks for unsupported data automation before validating the workflow.

Red signals:

- The user treats the product as a toy demo.
- The user cannot distinguish synthetic starter evidence from real source material.
- The user wants investment tips without source-backed reasoning.

## Launch Gate

Move to the next build only when real sources, reviewed memos, and customer demand appear in the same sprint.

Green gate:

- At least 3 paid-intent users.
- At least 50 real questions.
- At least 15 uploaded source packs.
- At least 3 reviewed memo packets.
- No synthetic evidence presented as real research.

If the gate is yellow, keep the pilot small and interview users. If the gate is red, improve positioning and source workflow before adding more features.
